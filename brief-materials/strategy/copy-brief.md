# Copy Brief

Project: Independence Phone Shopify build

Date: 2026-06-22

## Core Position

Independence Phone is the family-first phone for parents who want their child reachable without handing over the internet.

Primary line:

> Give them a phone. Not the internet.

Supporting line:

> A shared family phone for the smartphone-free years.

## Audience

Primary:

Parents of 8-13 year olds who want a way to reach their child but do not want the child's first phone to be a smartphone.

Secondary:

- Parents who miss the household landline model.
- Screen-time-conscious families.
- Parents comparing Bark, Gabb, Pinwheel, Troomi, watches, and dumb phones.
- Parents who are budget-sensitive and overwhelmed by device options.

## What The Buyer Is Really Hiring

They are not hiring a gadget. They are hiring relief from a parenting conflict:

> My kid needs more independence, but I do not want apps, web access, social feeds, and phone drama to arrive with it.

The copy should make parents feel sane for wanting a simpler step.

## Messaging Hierarchy

### 1. Parent Conflict

Lead with the tradeoff:

- Kids need independence.
- Parents need reachability.
- Smartphones bring more than calling.

Primary copy:

> Your child may be ready to call home. That does not mean they are ready for the internet in their pocket.

### 2. Product Mechanism

Make the product obvious:

- It is a phone that acts like a phone.
- It is for calling and connection.
- It is not a restricted smartphone.

Primary copy:

> Independence Phone gives families the calling layer first, without the apps, feeds, browser, or app store parents are trying to delay.

### 3. Use Cases

Name concrete buying moments:

- Home-alone minutes.
- Bus days.
- After-school activities.
- Sleepovers.
- Grandparents.
- Emergency contact.
- The kitchen-counter family phone.

Primary copy:

> For bus days, home-alone minutes, grandparents, and the years before a smartphone.

### 4. Product Choice

Keep the choice simple:

- Freedom Phone: everyday family phone.
- Patriot Phone: rugged family phone.

Primary copy:

> Choose the everyday model or the rugged model. Both keep the focus on calling.

### 5. Service And Price

Be transparent:

- Hardware price.
- Monthly service.
- Annual service.
- Add-ons.
- Shipping.
- Taxes.

Primary copy:

> Start with the phone, add simple monthly service, and choose only the features your family actually needs.

### 6. Trust Layer

Use patriotism after the parent understands the product:

- American-owned.
- 42 years in communications.
- 250th anniversary package.

Primary copy:

> American-owned and family-focused, backed by decades of communication experience.

## Voice And Tone

Use:

- Plainspoken.
- Calm.
- Practical.
- Parent-to-parent.
- Warm but not sentimental.
- Confident but not absolute.

Avoid:

- Fear-mongering.
- Shaming parents who buy smartphones.
- Political-first language.
- Overpromising safety.
- Tech jargon.
- "AI monitoring" language.
- Calling it a kid smartphone.

## Copy Guardrails

- Do not claim cellular mobility unless confirmed.
- Do not claim SMS, GPS, camera, 911, or emergency functionality unless confirmed.
- Do not claim "unhackable" or "impossible to bypass."
- Do not attack competitors by name on the main page.
- Do not say "eliminate spam calls" unless Auto Attendant truly supports that outcome; use "help screen spam calls" if needed.
- Do not hide recurring service cost.
- Do not overuse patriotic language before explaining the product.
